GATE / University Exam Question:

"If a Moore machine has m states and n output symbols, what is the maximum number of states in the equivalent Mealy machine?"

Answer: At most m states (exactly m states in standard conversion).
Explanation: Since each Moore state $q_j$ has a fixed output $\lambda_M(q_j)$, every arrow entering $q_j$ simply takes $\lambda_M(q_j)$. No state splitting is required! (Unlike Mealy-to-Moore where states can expand up to $m \times n$).
